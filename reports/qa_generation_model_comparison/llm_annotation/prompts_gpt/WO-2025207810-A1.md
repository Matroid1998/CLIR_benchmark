# Question quality review — anonymised systems

You are reviewing candidate retrieval-benchmark questions written about a chemistry patent passage.

Each record is **one patent document** with **several candidate questions**
(**Q1–Q6**), each with its own answer span, generated by different systems. The
systems are **anonymised** and the order is **randomised for every document**,
so Q1 is *not* always the same system. Judge every question **independently, on
its own merits** — do not try to guess which system wrote it.

For each of **Q1–Q6** you score the faithfulness and quality criteria and decide
whether to keep the question:

- **Faithfulness (1–5, required)** — grounding, precision, and numerical
  fidelity — checks whether that question's **own answer** is supported by the
  **Source passage**.
- The document's **Mode** (shown in **Context**) tells you which quality block
  to fill:
  - **technical** documents → fill the **[technical]** criteria
    (search-bar realism, specificity, phrasing economy, focus).
  - **semantic** documents → fill the **[semantic]** criteria
    (search realism, lexical distance, conceptual framing, retrievability).
  Leave the block for the *other* mode blank.
- **Linguistic quality (1–5)** and **Keep this question?** apply to every
  question and are **required**.
- At the **end** there is a single **optional note** for the whole document —
  use it to flag ties, a stand-out question, or a problem shared by several.

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each)

**Faithfulness (all modes)**
- *grounding* — Is the answer fully supported by a contiguous span of the passage? 5 = directly extracted, 1 = not in the passage.
- *precision* — Does the answer avoid unsupported details or padding? 5 = no padding, 1 = mostly padding.
- *numerical fidelity* — Are numbers, units, and ranges reproduced exactly? 5 = exact, 1 = invented or contradicted. Choose 5 if there are no numbers.

**Technical mode**
- *search-bar realism* — does it read like a real search-bar query?
- *specificity* — would this query narrow to the relevant document, or match
  arbitrarily?
- *phrasing economy* — is the question paraphrased rather than lifted from the
  source?
- *focus* — does the question ask for exactly one fact?

**Semantic mode**
- *search realism* — does it read like a real retrieval query for a document
  like this one?
- *lexical distance* — is the question meaningfully paraphrased from the
  passage's vocabulary? (higher = less keyword overlap)
- *conceptual framing* — does the question target a concept rather than a fact?
- *retrievability* — would this query distinguish this passage from unrelated
  documents?

**Both modes**
- *linguistic quality* — is the question grammatical and fluent in its language?
- *Keep this question?* — would you keep it in the benchmark? Be willing to say
  no.

("yes"/"no"), and a one-sentence reason; plus the optional shared note
(empty string if you have nothing to add). Everything you need is in this

[EN] Title: Oligonucleotides, sequences, methods, and systems thereof for analyzing nucleic acid molecules

Abstract: The present disclosure relates to oligonucleotides and sequences, methods, and systems for improved NGS library preparation, including RNAseq library preparation. In addition, error-checking barcode nucleotide sequences based on Hamming codes are described.
