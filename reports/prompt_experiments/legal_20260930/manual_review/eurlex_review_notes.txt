Completed a source-grounded manual review of all 263 question/answer candidates across all 20 EUR-Lex documents and 40 target/mode families. Every A/B/C group, including abstentions, is represented in eurlex_reviews.json. The source text was read before the candidate sets; supplied references were consulted for wine operations, VAT conditions, Aegean supply arrangements and collective copyright management. No provider calls were made. No version mapping, old scores or prompt files were opened for this review.

Ratings concern the QUESTION: clarity, substantive usefulness, sufficient retrieval context, sound premises and source answerability. Supplied answers were assessed separately. Several strong questions have incomplete answers, so a high question rating with answer_status major is intentional. Examples include asking which remedy exists while receiving only an unresolved reference to paragraph 1, and asking about bottling records while receiving only a general obligation to record operations. Legitimate hypothetical facts, ordinary rule application, necessary legal terminology and useful yes/no questions were accepted. No fixed length, date, anchor-count or protagonist requirement was applied.

The blinded groups often tie. Many differences are small wording or scope changes rather than a general superiority of direct questions or scenarios. The clearer improvements involve identifying the actual regime, preserving a decisive subtype, asking for a genuinely unknown proposition, and avoiding document-dependent placeholders. Because labels were randomized independently for each document/mode, an overall A/B/C winner would be meaningless. Version-level conclusions require the parent agent to join this completed review to the withheld mapping.

Concrete matched comparisons:

32014L0059 financial-collateral protection, fact_pattern: groups A and B consistently specify title-transfer financial collateral or netting and the need to ensure covered-deposit availability. C2 says only financial collateral, which is broader than the protected title-transfer category and leaves a decisive subtype missing. This is a substantive question-scope defect, not a request for extra story detail. Conversely, lookup B1 remains a good question even though its answer omits the necessity-for-deposit-availability qualification.

32005R1572 rye tender, fact_pattern: A2 and B1 identify the first partial invitation. C1 instead says the trader is preparing its first tender. A trader may enter a later round, so the first-invitation date cannot be inferred from a personal first bid. C2's dated question about an excluded week and C3's receiving-body question are sound. The preference reflects the whole set, not only its best candidate.

32013R1381 programme funding, fact_pattern: A1/A2 and B2 omit the programme even though the applicable category or beneficiary obligation depends on it. C1 and C3 identify the Rights, Equality and Citizenship Programme. C2, however, asks which support concerns an NGO implementing an action with European added value and receives that same activity back; its information need is thin. All three lookup groups are strong, showing that the differences arise from specific wording and context rather than a blanket mode preference.

32014R0788 maritime recognised organisations: fact_pattern A identifies ship inspection/survey organisations; B and C leave recognised organisation and Commission decision generic. In lookup, several remedy-information questions largely restate the duty to inform about judicial remedies, rather than asking a substantive unknown. A and B are therefore mixed rather than unqualified winners.

32006L0112 Finnish/Swedish VAT: A1 and B1/B2 in fact_pattern ask category membership. They do not need facts proving ultimate exemption eligibility. They are strong questions. C's direct category-list questions are also strong; only C3's listed exemptions reference and implicit VAT context are minor question weaknesses. In lookup, A1 broadens the conditions question to the common VAT system generally, and C3 refers to unspecified supplies. B's two category-list questions maintain clearer scope. These judgments were based on Article 111 and its supplied Article 110 reference, not native scores.

32014L0026 copyright collective management: fact_pattern A/B identify the regulated activities and category distinctions. C repeatedly uses unspecified provisions or omits the copyright setting. Lookup A2 gives the full organisation category within the question that asks which organisations qualify, making it circular; B and C preserve genuine scope questions.

32012R0966 budget delegation: only fact_pattern C generates questions. They are substantive and source-answerable even though they are direct institutional queries rather than client scenarios. In lookup, B is naturally phrased; C repeatedly prefixes an article-heading-like Under Delegation of budget implementation powers. The latter is a small language issue, not an answerability failure.

Separate scenario-diversity observation: strong question ratings do not imply successful fact-pattern generation. Timestamp fact_pattern A, gas nominations C, arbitration A, maritime organisations A, indirect-action access B, budget delegation C, wine registers B, Finnish/Swedish VAT C, Aegean supply arrangements C and copyright management C contain direct or mostly direct rule questions. They were not penalized solely for this. If these groups belong to one version, that version may improve question quality partly by abandoning the intended scenario format. Scenario conformity and retrieval-quality ratings must be reported separately.

Abstentions: three documents have no candidates in either mode, and the deltamethrin document has no fact-pattern candidates. Empty groups are unscored, not zero. A useful generated administrative question is not rejected merely because earlier mode rules might call implementation clauses boilerplate. The deltamethrin lookup question was assessed on its actual specificity and answerability.

Limitations: this is one agent's qualitative reading of a purposive diagnostic subset, not independent human expert adjudication or a representative estimate of full-corpus quality. Some timestamp and VAT wording was encountered in earlier case audits before this blinded assignment; the hidden mapping remained closed, but prior familiarity prevents a claim of perfect reviewer blinding. Candidate groups are not always aligned on the same proposition, and group sizes vary. Ratings distinguish question quality from answer quality, but they do not measure actual retrieval success. Best candidate IDs select the first equally highest-rated candidate when several tie; preferred_labels reflects the full question set, with ties where no meaningful overall difference is apparent. No version-level conclusion has been made before unblinding.
