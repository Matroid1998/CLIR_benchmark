Blind manual question review: UN packet A, document indices 7–9

Scope: A/RES/62/137#12; A/RES/68/18#1; S/RES/2140(2014)#13. All 70 candidates in 9 document/mode families and 27 groups reviewed. Read targets before candidates and consulted source context for identity, scope and status. No private mapping, prompt versions or automated scores consulted; no model/provider calls. A/B/C labels are independently randomized by family and are not version identities.

Matched findings:
- Beijing follow-up: lookup A and B are both strong, despite different candidate counts. Practitioner C 2 clearly identifies Beijing follow-up, actors and stages; C 1's 'this General Assembly follow-up' leaves its antecedent missing. Practitioner B's 'required'/'had to' wording is less faithful to an urging. Semantic A 1 seeks how concerns would shape conference preparation, but the source only states a general call to give them attention. Many otherwise good questions have answers that unnecessarily reproduce the entire event list; this is recorded separately from question quality.
- Least-developed-country graduation: lookup C is the strongest overall set. Lookup B 2 asks which UN entity would provide support, while the source names only the UN system collectively; no particular agency is supplied. Country-specific graduation questions are clearer when they state the category or identify the 2013 decision; an arbitrary date is not required for the well-defined continuity principle in practitioner A 3. Semantic B preserves the invitations and granted arrangements. Semantic C 1 has an ambiguous 2013 placement suggesting graduation itself then, although the text specifies preparatory years. Semantic C 2's preventing an abrupt graduation adds a purpose/outcome beyond the stated extra period. No educational-graduation interpretation was used as an automatic failure; the substantive issue is ambiguity among country-status regimes and decisions.
- Yemen sanctions: practitioner B has three consistently clear questions; missing an exact date alone did not lower B 1. Lookup B appropriately asks the full reporting schedule, while a narrower initial-deadline question may legitimately omit later discretionary reporting. Semantic A/B ask meaningful mechanisms supported by the mandate. Semantic C 1's 'How did ... engage States and respond' instead asks for historical action that the target does not report. Designation authority should not be confused with the Council's imposition of the measures or automatic designation of every potentially involved actor.

Preferences are based on the complete question set, with ties retained; answer-only overbreadth does not lower an otherwise good question. This is a qualitative agent review of an English diagnostic subset, not human expert validation, a measured retrieval study or a causal experiment. Differences between 4 and 5 are more discretionary than a clearly wrong status, unidentified reference or unsupported specificity. Hidden-version conclusions must wait for the parent to map labels.
