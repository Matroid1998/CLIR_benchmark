Why the legal prompts changed

Exact excerpts from frozen snapshots, paired with the revised rule. These are documented mechanisms and hypotheses under test—not a claim that the revised questions are already better.

Experiment design · Structured rationale and snapshot hashes

Routing is not quality. UN practitioner routing at this checkpoint: baseline: 6/50 completed Jev decisions; debiased: 44/50 completed Jev decisions. The 6→44 shift is a warning about overlapping mode definitions and backend sensitivity, not evidence that bias has been eliminated.

EUR-Lex: broad lookup eligibility competes with a narrower case test

Routing policy intervention

Original clause

Choose fact_pattern when a concrete situation with at least two legally relevant particulars naturally instantiates the target's conditions. A fictional actor or location added to a general request is not enough.

baseline: eurlex/decider/jev · questions.mode.instructions.selection[0]

Choose lookup when the useful information need is a direct point of law and adding a case would mainly decorate it or require unsupported assumptions.

baseline: eurlex/decider/jev · questions.mode.instructions.selection[1]

Revised clause

TIE POLICY: when both modes support good questions and the case passes the test, choose fact_pattern. The possibility of rewriting it as a direct lookup does not disqualify it. No mode quota or preferred frequency applies.

debiased: eurlex/decider/jev · questions.mode.instructions.selection[3]

A direct lookup can describe almost every legal rule, while a scenario must clear additional factual conditions. The original prompts already supported fact patterns, included positive examples and preferred a case when meaningful particulars fit; the baseline standard GPT selected 37/50 fact patterns while Jev selected 0/50. The revised tie rule strengthens that preference. It does not remove an explicit original lookup priority, and it does not establish that fact patterns are better.

Simplified version: The simplified router preserves this same tie policy; it is not an independent neutral policy.

EUR-Lex: routine application was easy to classify as penalized inference

Faithfulness rubric correction

Original clause

No inference whatsoever. (Rare.)

baseline: eurlex/faithfulness · prompt line 63

3 — Answer is grounded in the supplied text but requires one small, defensible inferential step — combining a definition with a value, or connecting a pronoun to its referent, or similar.

baseline: eurlex/faithfulness · prompt line 67

Revised clause

Hypothetical facts belong to the question and need not be stated in legislation. Matching these stipulated facts to an explicit category, condition, threshold or date, identifying an explicit breach, and resolving ordinary referents are direct application, not unsupported legal inference. They do not by themselves reduce grounding.

debiased: eurlex/faithfulness · prompt line 48

Fact patterns necessarily connect supplied hypothetical facts with a rule. The old scale could penalize that operation, or even pronoun resolution, despite correct evidence. The revised rule allows explicit condition/category matching while still excluding missing facts, converse rules and outside law.

Simplified version: Retains direct application and context resolution as permitted operations.

Both corpora: extreme brevity and rarity of 5s can distort faithfulness

Shared grading calibration

Original clause

5 is reserved for genuinely exceptional candidates with no identifiable weakness. If you can point to any imperfection, it is not a 5. 5s should be uncommon.

baseline: eurlex/faithfulness · prompt line 40

5 — Answer is the shortest possible span that fully answers the question. Not one extra word. No surrounding context, no hedges, no elaboration. (Rare.)

baseline: un/faithfulness · prompt line 80

Revised clause

Completeness outranks minimum word count. Retain conditions, exceptions, units, status and necessary attribution. Evidence deciding a yes/no query adds information even when it repeats query terms. Do not punish a necessary multi-clause span.

debiased: un/faithfulness · prompt line 14

Necessary qualifiers, governing clauses and attribution can make a complete answer longer. A rarity prior also moves scores without a candidate defect. Revised grading rewards complete, appropriately concise evidence and requires a specific reason for deductions.

Simplified version: Preserves completeness, necessary context and criterion-based scoring without a rarity quota.

EUR-Lex: case evidence need not repeat the whole legal conclusion

Clarification of an ambiguous contract

Original clause

Matching stated conditions and recognizing breach of an explicit duty or “only” restriction are allowed.

baseline: eurlex/quality/fact_pattern · prompt line 30

Require outcome-changing qualifications, but not unrelated conditions when only a category, value, or prerequisite is requested.

baseline: eurlex/quality/fact_pattern · prompt line 54

Revised clause

The answer is an evidence span read with the question and supplied source; a phrase or list item is sufficient when its governing clause supplies the legal relationship. Do not demand an added yes/no, a calculated date, or a rewritten case-specific conclusion. Do not penalize stipulated hypothetical facts for being absent from legislation.

debiased: eurlex/quality/fact_pattern · prompt line 60

The baseline quality verifier already permitted condition matching, rejected an added yes/no answer and waived unrelated entitlement conditions for category-only asks. The revision reinforces existing permissions and aligns them with the conflicting faithfulness rubric; it also addresses observed over-strict grading. It is not new permission for case application. A case still needs two relevant particulars and every fact decisive for its actual ask.

Simplified version: Keeps ordinary case application valid without demanding extra drama or an added yes/no conclusion.

UN: the practitioner stance excluded concrete requests and authorizations

Generation/verifier eligibility correction

Original clause

1. NORM-STATE STANCE. Ask what applied, was required/permitted, was due, or stood at a specified time—not what an organ said, decided, requested, authorized, reaffirmed, or continued to do. “What did the Security Council authorize?” fails; “What troop ceiling applied?” can pass.

baseline: un/quality/practitioner · prompt line 16

Revised clause

Questions may ask what applied, was required/permitted, what an identified proposal would change, what task was requested, or what a source reported. Preserve a proposal or request as such; do not require every source to establish binding law.

debiased: un/quality/practitioner · prompt line 18

The baseline already allowed attributed findings, figures and concrete events. Its norm-state rule nevertheless excluded specific speech/request framings, such as asking what an organ requested or authorized. That can reject a useful request/proposal question or encourage a stronger legal status than the evidence supports. The revision expands those framings while preserving their status; it does not newly introduce all reported facts.

Simplified version: Allows concrete requests, proposals, tasks and reported facts; it does not convert them into binding duties.

UN: anchor quotas plus a hard word ceiling create conflicting demands

Generation/verifier eligibility correction

Original clause

The question supplies the named regime, two substantive anchors and usable absolute time. Name the actual spans. Missing time, fewer than two anchors, unnamed regime or unresolved essential reference: at most 2.

baseline: un/quality/practitioner · prompt line 109

Wording & Length: direct interrogative, 8–20 words, hard maximum 25.

baseline: un/generation/practitioner · prompt line 25

Revised clause

Require sufficient substantive context and relevant time, not two separately countable anchors. A named composite operational setting can identify the regime without additional generic institutions.

debiased: un/quality/practitioner · prompt line 20

Prefer concise natural questions, usually 8–25 words. This is a guideline: retain necessary scope, attribution and qualifications even when longer.

debiased: un/generation/practitioner · prompt line 10

A single distinctive operational phrase can identify a need, while adding a second separately counted anchor, date and attribution can force awkward or truncated wording. Revised prompts test actual identification and concision rather than independent counts.

Simplified version: Preserves one sufficient composite anchor and a soft 8–25-word guideline.

UN: recurring wording was required to contain yearly novelty

Generation/verifier eligibility correction

Original clause

For these, the year alone is not enough when the paragraph is unchanged year to year: ask only about content specific to THIS instance — a date, a figure, a newly added task, a named event.

baseline: un/generation/practitioner · prompt line 13

Revised clause

Recurring wording is not automatically unusable: check whether the question identifies the relevant regime, reporting instance, measure or period. Do not assume unseen duplicates; do not require a norm to change every year to ask what applied then.

debiased: un/generation/practitioner · prompt line 6

A useful dated question about an unchanged norm can fail an instance-novelty test even when the regime and period are identified. The revision keeps specificity requirements but does not assume unseen duplicate passages or demand that a norm change annually.

Simplified version: Retains concrete recurring content and rejects unsupported assumptions about duplicates.

UN: guessed reader knowledge can suppress short, useful answers

Generation/verifier informativeness correction

Original clause

Guessability: a specialist could complete the answer from the question with better than even odds ("one-day" for the duration of informal hearings; "in writing" for the form of a request).

baseline: un/generation/practitioner · prompt line 20

Revised clause

A yes/no condition can genuinely be unknown. Do not infer its answer from a conventional-looking question or speculate about what a specialist knows. Only actual circularity or disclosed answers reduce informativeness.

debiased: un/quality/practitioner · prompt line 11

The old better-than-even-odds test is an unmeasured prior about specialists, rather than evidence of an exposed answer. Revised grading penalizes actual circularity and answer disclosure, while permitting a short deciding span or genuine yes/no need.

Simplified version: Keeps actual unknown information as the test; familiar-looking facts are not automatically uninformative.

UN semantic grading: clarify that extraction is an answer contract

Clarification, not a relaxation of answer support

Original clause

lexical_distance: Natural conceptual reformulation with meaning intact. One avoidable copied construction can warrant 4; substantially mirrored predicates/syntax: at most 3; heavy copied scaffolding or meaning-changing substitution: at most 2; near-verbatim question conversion: 1.

baseline: un/quality/semantic · prompt line 118

Revised clause

The answer is intentionally extractive: its overlap with the source is never a lexical-distance defect.

debiased: un/quality/semantic · prompt line 20

The baseline criterion already referred to question conversion, but observed notes sometimes criticized answer overlap. The revision explicitly restricts lexical-distance deductions to question scaffolding. It still rejects unsupported mechanisms, implementation claims and missing qualifications.

Simplified version: Explicitly says QUESTION scaffolding only and never penalizes copied ANSWER text.

UN routing still has an unresolved source-intent distinction

Remaining identification problem

Original clause

Lookup and practitioner may both fit precise content. Use the source-specific versus regime-specific distinction and the examples; neither an available identifier nor the possibility of omitting it decides the choice by itself.

baseline: un/decider/jev · questions.mode.instructions.selection[1]

Revised clause

Lookup and practitioner often fit the same facts. Choose lookup when identifying a particular source is naturally part of the need; choose practitioner when the regime/situation and time identify the need naturally without that locator. An available symbol does not make lookup preferable.

debiased: un/decider/jev · questions.mode.instructions.selection[1]

Lookup and practitioner can ask about the same fact with different source-locator wording. The decider receives a passage, not an actual searcher or their source knowledge. Broader practitioner eligibility can therefore reverse the routing preference without proving greater question quality. Both prompts retain this overlap.

Simplified version: Retains the known-source versus source-free specialist distinction; shorter wording cannot by itself identify unobserved user intent.

What the experiment can establish

  • Native quality criteria differ by mode and are edited between versions; higher native /40 scores are not independent cross-version quality evidence.
  • The frozen, blinded common evaluator uses the same five criteria and usability gate across all versions. Its results are required before any claim that a version improves quality.
  • The fixed diagnostic sample is English only: 50 EUR-Lex articles and 50 UN passages, with 25 UN resolutions, 20 meeting passages and 5 letters. It is stratified, not a representative estimate of full-corpus or multilingual routing frequencies.
  • There is one generation draw per target/mode/version. Routing counts are not a success criterion, and best-of-batch score comparisons must show candidate availability and usable coverage.
  • The revised bundle changes several generation, routing and grading rules together. The simplified bundle compresses that contract; this is not a single-clause causal ablation.
