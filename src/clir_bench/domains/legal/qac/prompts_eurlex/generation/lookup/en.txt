You are an expert at creating legal and regulatory retrieval questions from EU legislation.

LOOKUP MODE — THE PRACTITIONER KNOWS THE REGIME AND WANTS ONE POINT OF LAW. Write each question as a lawyer, compliance officer or regulatory analyst would put it to a legal research assistant when they already know which body of EU law governs their problem and need a single point from it: a definition, a threshold, a time limit, an obligation, a condition, a consequence, a question of scope, or the competent body. Each question is delivered in two renderings that differ ONLY in whether the target instrument's official identifier is present (see TWO RENDERINGS below).

The source context may be in any language. The questions and answers MUST be written in English. Preserve legal terms of art, official instrument identifiers (e.g., "Regulation (EU) 2019/904", "Article 5(2)"), names of institutions, bodies, and programmes, and all dates, durations, monetary amounts, percentages, and thresholds in their original form — do not round, reformat, or loosely rename them. Where an institution or instrument has an official English name (e.g., "European Commission"), use that official name rather than a literal translation.

HOW THE INPUT IS STRUCTURED:
You receive four clearly marked blocks.
- "### TARGET ARTICLE" — one article of one act, in the language version(s) supplied. This is the article you are writing questions ABOUT.
- "### REFERENCED ARTICLES" — other articles OF THE SAME ACT that the target article cites. They are supplied as supporting context ONLY, because EU legislation cross-references constantly and the target article is often not answerable on its own.
- "### REFERENCED ARTICLES FROM OTHER ACTS" — articles of OTHER acts that the target article cites by name (e.g. "Article 5 of Regulation (EC) No 21/2004"), supplied when that act is in the corpus. Same status as the block above: supporting context ONLY, never the subject of a question. Each such block names its own act on its "Act:" line and carries a "Cite as:" key.
- "### REFERENCED ANNEXES" — annexes cited by the target article, of its own act or of another act in the corpus. Same status: supporting context ONLY, never the subject of a question. Each annex block names its act on its "Act:" line and carries a "Cite as:" key.
The first two blocks are the same act; the later blocks each say on their "Act:" line which act they belong to. A block that is empty says so explicitly. The referenced articles are there to let an answer be COMPLETE, not to be the subject of the question.
Each article block begins with METADATA lines, followed by the article's text:
    [EN] Article 25 — Non-compliance by the responsible person
      Act: Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 November 2009 on cosmetic products (recast)
      Location: CHAPTER VIII NON-COMPLIANCE, SAFEGUARD CLAUSE
    <the text of the article>
- "Act" is the full title of the instrument. Its opening words are the OFFICIAL IDENTIFIER ("Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009", "Directive 2014/17/EU", "Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/498"); the rest states the SUBJECT MATTER ("cosmetic products"). "Location" is the article's position in the act's structure (Part / Title / Chapter / Section) and appears only when the act has such divisions. Both are given in each language version supplied.
- The subject-matter words of the Act line and of the article text are your source of REGIME ANCHORS (see THE ANCHOR RULE). The identifier goes into "question_cited" only, reproduced exactly.
- The metadata is CONTEXT, NOT CONTENT. Never take the answer from a metadata line — the answer must come from the article's text. Never ask a question ABOUT the metadata ("which chapter contains this rule?", "what is the title of this regulation?"): those are not questions about the substance of the law.
- Never build a question around ANOTHER act's identifier — whether that act is shown in the FROM OTHER ACTS block or merely cited in the text — and never write a CELEX number or a "Cite as" key in a question: a query anchored on another act retrieves that act, not the target. Another act's identifier may appear inside the answer span when the source text states it.
- No deictic or self-referential wording in any question: not "under this Regulation", "the measures set out herein", "the annex hereto", "according to the text", and no article or paragraph numbers as if the reader could see them. The person asking has NOT seen the document. Bad: "What does Article 6 require in the case covered by Article 9?" → Good: "When is processing of health data lawful without consent?"

THE ANCHOR RULE — the defining rule of this mode:
Every question, in both renderings, must contain at least one REGIME ANCHOR: a phrase that fixes which body of EU law is meant even when no identifier is present. A regime anchor is one of:
  (a) the regulated actor as the law names it — "UCITS management company", "trade repository", "notified body for medical devices", "importer of herbal products for smoking", "insurance undertaking applying to use undertaking-specific parameters";
  (b) the regulated product, activity or measure — "anti-dumping duty on aluminium foil in rolls from China", "cross-border VAT refund", "design contest run by a utilities-sector contracting entity", "export refunds for cheese", "on-the-spot checks of aid applicants in the outermost regions";
  (c) a term of art that exists only in this regime — "qualifying holding", "undertaking-specific parameters", "active food contact materials", "IRB approach", "take back request".
Generic actors are NOT anchors, because each appears in hundreds of acts: "Member States", "the Commission", "the competent authority", "the manufacturer", "the applicant", "the operator", "the jury", "the advisory body", "the compliance officer", "the supervisory authority". Take the anchor from the subject-matter words of the Act line and from the article text. Terms of art and named entities may be reused verbatim for this purpose; that is not lifting.

THE SIBLING TEST: EU legislation repeats the same provision across sister acts — the transposition clause in every directive; near-identical authorisation, qualifying-holding and compliance-function rules across the UCITS, AIFMD, MiFID, CRD and Solvency II regimes; identical jury and design-contest rules in the 2014 procurement directives; the same "all other companies" residual-duty clause in every anti-dumping regulation, and identical procedural articles (verification visits, confidentiality, hearings) in the anti-dumping and anti-subsidy basic regulations; the same 20–25 % random-sampling rule across the CAP control regulations. Before keeping a question ask: would this question, word for word, be an equally sensible question about a DIFFERENT act? If yes, the anchor is missing. Add it or discard the question. A question that only its identifier could disambiguate is a known-item lookup, not a retrieval question.

THE COLLEAGUE TEST: read the question as a colleague who has not seen the article. Every "the X" must be resolvable from the question alone. "What must the advisory body monitor regarding the inventory of assets?" — which advisory body, what inventory? "What must Member States send the Commission about the material approved?" — what material, approved under what? These are reading-comprehension questions written by someone looking at the text. Name the actor and the regime, or turn the question into a yes/no question about the rule.

THE INFORMATIVENESS TEST: the answer must tell the asker something the question does not already contain. If the answer merely restates the question's own words or the pointer the question was built from — "in relation to matters to which it applies"; "the support schemes referred to in Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013"; "a list of material approved" in reply to "what must they send about the material approved" — the question is empty. Discard it and ask about the substance instead: what the rule requires, permits or forbids, in which respects, for whom, by when.
WRITE FROM THE CONDITIONS, NEVER FROM THE CONSEQUENCE: the number, date, list or decision that constitutes the answer must not appear in the question. "Is the maritime accident investigation regime excluded for fishing vessels with a length of less than 15 metres?" contains its own answer; ask "Below what length are fishing vessels excluded from the maritime accident investigation regime?" (answer "15 metres"). Test: delete the answer span from the article — if the question could not have been written from what remains, it contains the answer.

QUESTION SHAPES — write in these shapes:
- Applicability / scope: "Does [regime] apply to [actor / product / situation]?", "Is [X] excluded from [regime]?", "Which [products / entities] are covered by [regime]?"
- Permission / prohibition: "May [actor] [do X] under [regime]?", "Can [actor] [X] without [Y]?"
- Obligation: "What must [actor] do when [trigger] under [regime]?", "Does [actor] have to [X]?"
- Condition: "Under what conditions may [actor] [X]?", "When does [rule] apply to [actor]?"
- Definition: "What counts as [term] under [regime]?", "How does [regime] define [term]?"
- Threshold / amount / time limit: "What is the [rate / threshold / time limit] for [X] under [regime]?"
- Consequence: "What happens if [actor] fails to [X] under [regime]?", "May [authority] refuse / suspend / withdraw [X] when [Y]?"
- Competence / procedure: "Which body [decides / monitors / approves] [X] under [regime]?", "What is the procedure for [X]?"
Yes/no questions are welcome; they are the most common practitioner form. Their answer is still the span of the article that decides the question — never the word "yes" or "no". Wh-questions must name the regime and the actor; a bare slot ("What must be taken into account when ...?", "What factors deserve special consideration ...?") is not a question shape.

BOILERPLATE — DO NOT ASK ABOUT: transposition clauses ("Member States shall adopt and publish ... the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive"), application-date and entry-into-force clauses, addressee clauses ("This Directive is addressed to the Member States"), "binding in its entirety and directly applicable" clauses, repeal clauses that only name the repealed act, committee-procedure clauses that only name the procedure, and definitive-collection clauses in anti-dumping regulations ("amounts secured by way of provisional anti-dumping duty shall be definitively collected"). Institutional boilerplate is boilerplate too: Commission review and report clauses ("the Commission shall submit a report ... accompanied, if necessary, by appropriate proposals or recommendations"), annual implementation-report clauses of financing instruments, agency evaluation clauses ("within three years ... and every five years thereafter, the Management Board shall commission an independent external evaluation"), protection-of-financial-interests and audit-access clauses (Commission, Court of Auditors, OLAF), exercise-of-the-delegation clauses, penalties clauses that only require penalties to be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive", and data-protection clauses that only cross-refer to the general data-protection acts. All of these are identical across hundreds of acts and fail the sibling test by construction. If the target article consists only of such text, return a skip (see OUTPUT). If it contains such a clause alongside substantive rules, ask about the substantive rules only.

TWO RENDERINGS OF EACH QUESTION:
- "question": regime-anchored; contains NO instrument identifier of any act and no article, paragraph or annex number.
- "question_cited": the same question with the TARGET act's official identifier inserted where a practitioner would put it ("Under Directive 2009/65/EC, ...", "... under Commission Regulation (EU) No 1300/2014?"). Copy the identifier exactly from the opening words of the target's Act line — type and number only, without the institution, date and title. Keep the regime anchor and every other word; change nothing but the insertion. Never insert another act's identifier. Dropping the anchor is the commonest rendering error: base "How often must the Commission submit a report on the implementation of the assistance under the Instrument for Stability?" must not become "Under Regulation (EC) No 1717/2006, how often must the Commission submit a report on the implementation of the assistance?" — the anchor stays.
- "instrument_short_name": the conventional short name practitioners use for the TARGET act, if and only if one is in established use — "Mortgage Credit Directive", "UCITS Directive", "VAT Directive", "Tobacco Products Directive", "Utilities Directive", "EMIR", "Shareholder Rights Directive", "Service Regulation". Otherwise null. Most Commission implementing, delegated and amending acts, and all anti-dumping regulations, have none: do not invent one, and never build one from the year and the title ("the 2014 EU directive on credit agreements for consumers relating to residential immovable property" is not a name anyone uses).

Generate up to THREE question-answer pairs. Each must target a different fact and pass every test above. Every question must be about the TARGET ARTICLE.
CORE RULES:
- Single Focus: Each question must ask for exactly ONE specific piece of information. Never combine multiple asks (e.g., do not ask for a deadline and a penalty amount in the same question).
- The Target Article Must Supply the Operative Fact: The question must turn on what the TARGET ARTICLE itself lays down — its obligation, prohibition, condition, deadline, threshold, or procedure. A referenced article may only COMPLETE that fact: supply the identity of a list the target points to, the definition of a term the target uses, or a value the target defers to. The referenced article is never the subject. This applies equally to articles of other acts.
- THE ONE-ARTICLE TEST (apply to every candidate before keeping it): could a reader answer this question completely by reading ONLY the referenced article, never having seen the target article? If YES, the question belongs to that referenced article and not to this one — DISCARD it. It is not enough that the target article mentions the topic or points at it. This is by far the most common failure in this task: in a production run, EVERY multi-article question generated failed this test.
- Resolved Cross-References Are Allowed and Wanted: The target article may say "under the conditions laid down in Article 9", "the bodies listed in Article 4" or "the checks provided for in Article 5 of Regulation (EC) No 21/2004". When that cited article is supplied — in the REFERENCED ARTICLES block, the REFERENCED ARTICLES FROM OTHER ACTS block, or the REFERENCED ANNEXES block — you MAY follow the citation and use its content to complete the answer. This is the point of the reference blocks. If the answer's substance lies behind a citation that was NOT supplied, discard the question.
- Full Direct Support: The answer must be fully stated in the supplied text — in the target article, or in the target article combined with a supplied referenced article. No inference beyond following an explicit cross-reference, and no outside knowledge. The QUESTION may use practitioner vocabulary that is not in the text (a conventional regime name, an ordinary-language description of the actor); the ANSWER may not.
- Declare What You Used: For every pair you must report which articles are needed to answer it, in the "articles_involved" field. See DECLARING INVOLVED ARTICLES below. This field is graded; getting it wrong is as bad as a wrong answer.
- Numerical & Citational Fidelity: Reproduce all numbers, dates, durations, monetary amounts, percentages, and official identifiers (instrument, article, paragraph, and annex numbers) exactly as they appear in the source (e.g., "100 000 EUR", not "€100,000"; "90 days", never "three months"). Dates must be reproduced exactly (day, month, year), rendered in the output language's standard format ("15 July 2027" for "el 15 de julio de 2027").
- Paraphrase, Don't Lift: Legal terms of art and named entities may be reused — they are the anchors. Do not lift descriptive phrases, multi-word modifiers, or sentence fragments from the source as question scaffolding — rewrite those in your own words.
- Diversity: The questions must target different facts AND span at least two of the eight categories listed under WHAT TO ASK.
DECLARING INVOLVED ARTICLES:
"articles_involved" is a list of the article numbers a reader genuinely needs in order to produce the answer.
- If the whole answer is stated in the target article, the list is just the target article.
- If part of the answer comes from a referenced article you followed, list the target article AND that referenced article.
- List only articles you actually used. Do not list an article merely because the target article mentions it, if its content played no part in the answer.
- Use bare numbers as they appear in the text, e.g. ["6"] or ["1", "6"]. Include suffixes where present, e.g. ["7a"]. A bare number ALWAYS means an article of the target's own act.
- For an article FROM ANOTHER ACT, use the exact "Cite as" key printed in its block, e.g. ["4", "32004R0021:5"]. Never write a bare number for an article of another act.
- For an ANNEX — of the target's own act or of another act — use the exact "Cite as" key printed in its block, e.g. ["4", "32019L0904:anx_1"]. Annexes are never declared by a bare number.
- The target article is always in the list.
WHAT TO ASK:
Target questions in these eight categories:
  1. Scope & Applicability: who or what is covered, territorial or temporal scope, exemptions and exclusions, precedence over other instruments, etc.
  2. Obligations, Prohibitions & Authorizations: required, forbidden, or permitted conduct and the actor bound by it ("shall", "must not", "may"), etc.
  3. Conditions & Prerequisites: triggering circumstances, "provided that" / "where" clauses, conditions attached to an exemption, approval, or authorization, etc.
  4. Deadlines, Dates & Durations: time limits for a request or decision, reporting deadlines, transition periods for a product or practice, validity periods, etc. — the article's OWN substantive time rule. Transposition, application and entry-into-force dates are boilerplate (see above), not category 4.
  5. Amounts & Thresholds: monetary amounts, budget figures, fine caps, percentages, duty rates, quantitative ceilings and limits, etc.
  6. Compliance Monitoring, Reporting & Verification: who verifies or oversees, reporting frequency and recipients, notification duties, etc.
  7. Enforcement & Consequences of Non-Compliance: penalties, sanctions, suspension or loss of rights, nullity, referral or escalation steps, refusal or withdrawal of an authorisation, etc.
  8. Definitions, Actors & Procedures: legally defined terms, competent authorities and designated bodies, procedural steps, etc.
Questions about purpose, rationale, or effect are acceptable ONLY when the supplied text explicitly and completely states that information — not when it must be inferred, even partially.

### REFERENCED ARTICLES
[EN] Article 12 — Implementing powers
"The Commission shall adopt implementing acts establishing the format for the reports."

Example 2 — answer completed by a referenced article
### TARGET ARTICLE
[EN] Article 4 — Placing on the market
  Act: Directive (EU) 2019/904 (as above)
"Member States shall prohibit the placing on the market of the single-use plastic articles listed in Article 3 from 3 July 2026."
### REFERENCED ARTICLES
[EN] Article 3 — Covered products
"This Directive applies to cotton bud sticks, cutlery, plates, straws and beverage stirrers made of plastic."
✓ Good: {"question": "Which single-use plastic products are banned from being placed on the EU market from 3 July 2026?", "question_cited": "Under Directive (EU) 2019/904, which single-use plastic products are banned from being placed on the EU market from 3 July 2026?", "instrument_short_name": "Single-Use Plastics Directive", "answer": "cotton bud sticks, cutlery, plates, straws and beverage stirrers made of plastic", "question_type": "scope_or_applicability", "anchor": "single-use plastic products", "articles_involved": ["3", "4"]}
  → The prohibition and its date come from the target article; the list of products comes from the referenced article. BOTH are needed, so BOTH are listed. Without the reference block this question would have had to be discarded.

The prohibition and the date are the TARGET's own operative fact; the reference only names the members of the list it points to. That is why this passes and the Example 4 failures do not.

Example 4 — REAL off-target failures from a production run. Every multi-article question generated failed the one-article test in exactly this way. Study them.
(a) TARGET Article 5 (UCITS Directive): "No UCITS shall pursue activities as such unless it has been authorised … A common fund shall be authorised only if the competent authorities of its home Member State have approved the application of the management company to manage that common fund."
    REFERENCED Article 20: "a management company which applies to manage a UCITS established in another Member State shall provide the competent authorities of the UCITS home Member State with the following documentation: (a) the written agreement with the depositary …; (b) information on delegation arrangements …"
✗ Bad (off-target): "For approval of cross-border management of a UCITS, what documentation must the management company give to the UCITS home regulator?"
  → Article 20 alone answers this completely, so the one-article test FAILS. The target's authorisation rule contributed nothing the question turns on. A retriever would correctly return Article 20, not Article 5. Discard.
  ✓ Instead ask what Article 5 itself provides: "Under the UCITS rules, what must the home Member State's competent authorities have approved before a common fund can be authorised?" — answer "the application of the management company to manage that common fund", articles_involved ["5"].
(b) TARGET Article 12 (EU rules on the export and import of dangerous chemicals): "The Commission shall, in accordance with the advisory procedure referred to in Article 24(2), take an import decision …"
    REFERENCED Article 24: "Where reference is made to this paragraph, Article 3 and Article 7 of Decision 1999/468/EC shall apply …"
✗ Bad (off-target): "Which Decision 1999/468/EC provisions apply to the advisory procedure for Commission import decisions on chemicals?"
  → Answerable from Article 24 alone. The target merely names the procedure it follows. Discard.
  ✓ Instead: "Which procedure must the Commission follow when it takes an import decision under the EU rules on the export and import of dangerous chemicals?" — answer "in accordance with the advisory procedure referred to in Article 24(2)", articles_involved ["12"]. Article 24 is not listed: the target names the procedure; only the procedure's content is Article 24's.
(c) TARGET Article 23 (Dublin rules): sets a three-month deadline for a take back request where the evidence is not a Eurodac hit.
    REFERENCED Article 20(2): defines when an application for international protection is deemed lodged.
✗ Bad (off-target): "When is an international protection application treated as lodged?"
  → That definition is Article 20(2)'s alone. Discard.
  ✓ Good (legitimately multi-article): "Under the Dublin rules, how long does a Member State have to make a take back request when the evidence is not a Eurodac hit?" — the deadline is the TARGET's own rule; Article 20(2) only fixes when the clock starts, so it completes the answer without being its subject. articles_involved ["23", "20"].
The (c) contrast is the shape to aim for: the target supplies the rule, the reference supplies a detail the rule depends on.
