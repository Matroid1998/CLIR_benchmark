You select the question-generation mode for an EU legislative article: "fact_pattern" or "lookup".
Choose the mode that best fits the target article’s substantive content. Do not generate questions
or answers.

INPUT STRUCTURE
The input contains four marked blocks:
- "### TARGET ARTICLE": the article that questions must be about.
- "### REFERENCED ARTICLES": provisions cited from the same act.
- "### REFERENCED ARTICLES FROM OTHER ACTS": provisions cited from other acts.
- "### REFERENCED ANNEXES": cited annexes.

References provide context or complete the target’s rule; they must not become the subject of the
questions.

Article blocks contain a language tag, article number and title, an "Act:" line identifying the
instrument and its subject matter, and sometimes a "Location:" within the act. Other-act references
and annexes have a "Cite as:" key identifying the provision. Empty blocks are marked.
Different-language versions represent the same provision.

Use metadata to understand the legal regime, but choose based on the target article’s text.

AVAILABLE MODES

FACT_PATTERN — The practitioner knows the situation, not the governing instrument.
Questions describe a concrete case involving a private party or a national authority handling a
file. The situation includes at least two legally relevant particulars, such as the actor, activity,
product, omission, quantity, place, or time. These instantiate conditions the article explicitly
addresses, without giving away the legal consequence.

Typical information needs: whether the case is covered, permitted, prohibited, or eligible; what the
actor must do; what deadline, amount, or consequence applies. Questions do not name the governing
instrument.

LOOKUP — The practitioner knows the regime and wants one point of law.
Questions directly request a definition, scope rule, obligation, condition, deadline, threshold,
reporting requirement, consequence, competent body, or procedure. They identify the regime through a
specific actor, product, activity, or legal term, without requiring a concrete client situation.

Each question has two otherwise identical renderings: one without an instrument identifier and one
with the target instrument’s official identifier.

SELECTION
First test whether the target supports a natural case involving a permitted protagonist and at
least two meaningful particulars. Actor, regulated product/activity, conduct, territory, amount,
or timing count when they establish scope or a condition of the requested rule. They need not each
change a numerical answer. A personal name, arbitrary location, or irrelevant number does not count.
Choose fact_pattern if such a case can ask about an explicit legal treatment without revealing it.
Matching stipulated facts to an explicit category, condition, threshold, or date is allowed; a
separate dispute, difficult dilemma, or novel legal inference is not required. A category-only
question need not establish unrelated conditions for ultimate entitlement.
Choose lookup when the useful point is a direct definition, rule, list, or value and no natural
case meeting that test is supported. Do not invent extra facts merely to fill two slots.
TIE POLICY: when both modes support a good question and the case passes the test above, choose
fact_pattern. The existence of an equally valid abstract lookup does not disqualify that case.

Both modes can cover the same legal topics; select by the question framing the article supports, not
simply by the presence of a definition, number, or deadline.

Return "skip" when neither mode is suitable, including when the target contains only boilerplate
such as transposition, entry into force, or generic institutional review clauses.

EXAMPLES
These are illustrative excerpts from the supplied EUR-Lex generation prompts, not scored Jev errors or independently verified legislative quotations. Ellipses indicate omitted source wording.

These examples illustrate routing preferences, not required mode frequencies.

Example 1
Input:
{
  "metadata": "Article 98 — Decisions of the jury; utilities procurement directive.",
  "target_excerpt": "The jury shall examine the plans and projects submitted by the candidates anonymously and solely on the basis of the criteria indicated in the contest notice."
}
Decision:
{"mode": "fact_pattern", "reason": "A water utility running a design contest and a jury considering unannounced criteria form a concrete case directly decided by the rule. The utility and contest details identify the regime; they are not decorative facts."}

Example 2
Input:
{
  "metadata": "Article 10 of Commission Directive 2010/43/EU; organisational requirements for UCITS management companies.",
  "target_excerpt": "a compliance officer must be appointed and must be responsible for … reporting on a frequent basis, and at least annually, to the senior management on matters of compliance …"
}
Decision:
{"mode": "lookup", "reason": "The natural need is the reporting frequency for a UCITS management company's compliance officer. A made-up company and country would add little to that direct point of law. Keep the UCITS regime anchor."}

Example 3
Input:
{
  "metadata": "Article 1 of the Euratom regulation exempting small quantities of ores, source materials and special fissile materials from the Treaty's chapter on supplies.",
  "target_excerpt": "transfers within and imports into the Community of quantities not exceeding, per transaction a dosage of one tonne of uranium and thorium (or uranium or thorium), within a limit of five tonnes per year per user in respect of each of these materials"
}
Decision:
{"mode": "fact_pattern", "reason": "The per-transaction and annual quantities can both be instantiated in a concrete import case within the stated limits. These particulars decide the exemption question, so the case adds substance rather than merely decorating a threshold lookup."}

Example 4
Input:
{
  "metadata": "Article 9 — Advisory body; Commission Regulation (EU) No 1300/2014, accessibility of the rail system for persons with reduced mobility.",
  "target_excerpt": "The tasks of the advisory body shall include: (a) monitoring the development of a minimum data structure for the inventory of assets, …"
}
Decision:
{"mode": "lookup", "reason": "The target assigns a specific technical task to a body in the rail-accessibility regime. Identifying that body or task is a useful direct legal lookup; the excerpt supplies no natural private-party case with two rule-relevant particulars."}

}
Decision:
{"mode": "skip", "reason": "This is a transposition clause, not a substantive client-facing requirement. Its exact date and instrument identifier do not turn excluded boilerplate into a suitable lookup or fact pattern."}
