You are an expert at creating semantic retrieval questions for United Nations documents (resolutions, reports, letters, and meeting records).
THE INFORMATION NEED OF THIS MODE:
Every question is a query typed by a searcher who knows the SITUATION and what they need to find out, but not the corpus's vocabulary and not which instrument governs — a desk officer new to a file, a journalist, a researcher approaching a regime from outside its terminology. Their query carries meaning, not the documents' exact words; the retrieval system must match concept to text. Write from that need: a real question about a situation, the response to it, or the people affected — never a report of what a body or a speaker SAID, and never a reading-comprehension question about "what the passage says". This mode stays conceptual: it does not chase single data points, but it still needs a concrete anchor and a year, because a query that matches everything finds nothing.
LANGUAGE:
The questions and answers MUST be written in English. Preserve names of missions, organs, bodies, and programmes, established terms of art ("arms embargo", "asset freeze"), and all dates, durations, monetary amounts, percentages, and thresholds in their original form. Where an organ, body, or instrument has an official English name, use that official name rather than a literal translation.
PARAPHRASE WITHOUT DRIFT: distance comes from ordinary words for situations and people, never from renaming terms of art or official names, and never at the cost of the meaning. Legal and diplomatic text is sensitive to exact words — when unsure whether a word is load-bearing, keep it.
HOW THE INPUT IS STRUCTURED:
You receive three marked sections.
- "### TARGET BLOCK" — one contiguous passage (whole paragraphs) of one UN document. You write questions ABOUT this text.
- "### REFERENCED DOCUMENTS" — documents the target block CITES, supplied ONLY so you can UNDERSTAND what the citations refer to. Never a source of answers, never the subject of a question.
- "### DOCUMENT CONTEXT" — surrounding text OF THE SAME DOCUMENT: its opening and, for long documents, the passages around the target block.
The roles are strict:
- The ANSWER must be fully stated in the TARGET BLOCK. Not partly — fully.
- The supporting sections exist so you can UNDERSTAND the block — never so you can answer from them. USE them to resolve what the block leaves implicit: which mission "the Mission" is, which committee "the Committee" is, which State "the speaker" represents, who wrote the letter, and the calendar year of the meeting, report, or session. If any part of the answer's substance is stated only in a referenced document or only in the context, DISCARD the question.
- THE QUOTATION RULE: when the block QUOTES or restates a provision of ANOTHER instrument, a question about that provision belongs to the QUOTED instrument and is off-target here, even though its words sit inside the block. Ask instead about what THIS document does with it — what its author alleges, applies, reports, contests.
The target block begins with METADATA lines ([EN] Document: <symbol> / Title: <title>), followed by the text. Use them to know what the passage is about, when it is from, and whether it belongs to a recurring series. The metadata is CONTEXT, NOT CONTENT: never take an answer from it, never ask about it, and never copy the document symbol or a resolution number out of it into a question. The SUBJECT-MATTER words of the title ("the situation in Rwanda") MAY appear as natural topic words; the identifier may not.
DEFINING RULES OF THIS MODE:
- No Instrument Identifiers: never include resolution or decision numbers, document symbols, or paragraph numbers in the question. Identifiers may appear inside the answer span.
- Conceptual, not extractive: the question targets a situation, a mechanism, or an affected party. A single data point — a troop ceiling, a deadline, a budget caveat, a named official — belongs to another mode; if the block offers only such facts, skip with skip_reason "extractive only".
- THE YEAR IS MANDATORY: every question carries an absolute year, date, or dated period ("in 2009", "in spring 1994", "after the 2011 legislative elections"), taken from the block, the context, or the metadata. Without it, a question about a mission, a regime, or a recurring resolution has one answer per year and therefore none.
- STANCE: never a speech report. Banned forms: "How did [State/body] describe...", "What did [body] say about...", "What caution/warning did [body] give...", "What did [body] stress/emphasize...", "What did the speaker say...". Ask about the situation, the response, or the affected party itself. The one legitimate attributed form is a STATE's POSITION on a concrete matter — a proposal, a measure, an action: "What position did Guyana take on the use of force in Kosovo in 1999?" — never its characterization of a situation ("how did Guyana describe the crisis").
LEXICAL DISTANCE — WHERE IT COMES FROM AND WHERE IT MUST NOT:
Actively paraphrase: a semantic retriever should match on meaning, not keyword overlap. Distance comes from describing the SITUATION, RESPONSE, or PEOPLE in ordinary words instead of the document's formulas — "the mass killing of civilians" for "the very numerous killings of civilians"; "UN peacekeepers" for the mission's formal designation; "people who fled across the border" for "refugees who crossed the border". Distance must NOT come from substituting terms of art or official names: "arms embargo" is not "weapons ban", "asset freeze" is not "account blocking", an International Day or a body keeps its name. A substituted term of art changes what is being asked about. Paraphrase must also never strip the discriminating anchor: keep the country, the mission, and the year.
NAMING FOR RETRIEVABILITY:
The person typing has NOT seen this document and is searching among hundreds of thousands of UN documents that share the same formulaic register.
- Every question MUST carry at least one concrete anchor: a country or situation ("Rwanda", "the conflict in Darfur"), a mission or operation ("UNAMIR"), a named body or actor type ("the Panel of Experts", "troop-contributing countries"), or a specific subject matter tied to a situation ("arms embargo exemptions for humanitarian equipment in Somalia"). A bare topic ("counterterrorism measures", "climate action", "developing countries", "human rights learning") is not an anchor. Report the anchor in "anchor" as a VERBATIM substring of the question.
- Every definite description must be NAMED in the question itself: never bare "the Committee", "the Regional Centre", "the speaker", "the sides", "the Council" — resolve the name from the block, the context, or the metadata.
- Never write "this resolution", "the present report", "this document", or refer to the document's contents as if visible.
- Test every question: typed into a search engine by a stranger, would it lead to THIS document's subject matter? "What did the Security Council express grave concern about?" fails. "How did the Security Council respond to attacks on displaced civilians in Rwanda in 1994?" passes.
- RECURRING SERIES — know the families: session-numbered General Assembly resolutions on standing agenda items (a country's human rights situation, the right to food, the right to development, human rights learning, the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions, New Caledonia); the annual ECOSOC and Assembly resolutions on the Occupied Palestinian Territory; the biennial "cooperation between the United Nations and [regional organization]" resolutions, whose consultation clauses are cloned across the OSCE, CIS, SCO, EurAsEC and every other partner organization; mandate renewals; International Day resolutions; periodic reports. An unchanged paragraph in such a series fits every year — and every partner organization — equally, even with the year attached. Prefer content specific to THIS instance — a named event, a new task, a figure; if the block holds only unchanged recurring text, skip with skip_reason "recurring boilerplate".
CONSEQUENTIAL ANSWER SPANS:
- QUALIFYING spans: measures established, extended, or modified; mandates and their scope; conditions, criteria, and exemptions; how a mechanism operates; who is bound, protected, or assisted and how; concrete reported events, findings, and stated rationales; and — for situation-framed questions — preambular ASSESSMENTS of the situation that state concretely what is happening ("delays by the signatory parties and the absence of an inclusive political settlement", "an estimated 250,000 refugees crossed the border").
- DISQUALIFIED spans, in any part of the document, INCLUDING their softer forms: commends; welcomes ("welcomes the continuing role of"); expresses appreciation or concern ("remains a cause for serious concern"); takes note; notes with appreciation; stresses or underlines the importance of; recognizes the primary responsibility of; reaffirms or reiterates commitment; encourages or invites in general terms; invites two officials to hold regular consultations; calls upon bodies to support, cooperate and collaborate with stakeholders; calls upon States to consider; calls upon "the international community"; aspirations. Preambular RECITALS — recalling, reaffirming, recognizing responsibility, expressing concern — are framing, not answer material, even though preambular ASSESSMENTS are.
- THE BOILERPLATE TEST: if the answer span could appear unchanged in dozens of other UN documents (stakeholder invitation lists, cooperate-and-collaborate lists, the universal "measures must comply with international law" clause, "primary responsibility lies with the authorities themselves", the standard consultations clause of every cooperation-with-organization resolution), it cannot anchor a question. When a block holds both ceremony and substance, ask about the substance.
ATTRIBUTION:
Much of this corpus reports what someone SAID. When the block attributes a statement, claim, estimate, or assessment, the question and answer must preserve the attribution — to the STATE or OFFICE ("the representative of South Africa", "the Secretary-General", "according to UN reporting in spring 1994"), never a bare personal name. An AGENTLESS PASSIVE or a claim stated as fact is stripped attribution: "as weapons kept reaching the separatists" presents Georgia's assertion as fact — write "according to Georgia" or "in Georgia's account". Attributed content is askable only when it is a position on an identifiable proposal, instrument, or measure, a stated rationale, or a concrete situation assessment — never an aspiration, a courtesy, or a generic exhortation.
- OWN ACTS NEED NO HEDGE: when a letter or report describes a concrete, completed act of its author's own side — France's letter recounting that the European Union adopted a disarmament plan endorsed by its Heads of State — the act may be asked about directly; the attribution requirement targets contested claims and characterizations of others, not an author's account of its own initiative.
THE INFORMATIVENESS TEST:
The answer must tell the asker something the question does not already contain AND could not have supplied. If the answer merely restates the question's own words or the pointer it was built from, or a reader could guess it from the question with better than even odds, the question is empty — discard it and ask about the substance instead. The answer span must also be self-contained: a pointer span ("the above priorities", "as adopted in the present resolution") answers nothing outside the document.
CORE RULES:
- THE BLOCK TEST (apply to every candidate): could a reader produce the COMPLETE answer from the TARGET BLOCK alone, using the references and context only to understand referring expressions, names, dates, and citations? If any needed fact, figure, list, or reason sits only in a reference or the context, DISCARD.
- Off-Target: a question answerable entirely from a referenced document is about THAT document — discard. Citations never complete answers.
- Grounded Answer: even though the question is conceptual, the answer must be a short contiguous span of the TARGET BLOCK that directly addresses it. Prefer short spans; if the only span is a whole paragraph, narrow the question or discard.
- Single Intent: one clear informational goal per question; broader than a single fact is fine, bundled unrelated asks are not.
- Wording & Length: natural search phrasing, 8–20 words, hard maximum 25.
- Diversity: the questions must target distinguishable aspects and span at least two of the framings: situation, response, stakeholder.
- SKIP RATHER THAN PAD: generate up to THREE pairs; fewer if fewer pass every rule. If the block supports none, return exactly [{"skip_reason": "<one short phrase>"}]. Never manufacture a question to reach three.
- Try to paraphrase. Don't repeat every single item and word mentioned in the text. But don't paraphrase in a way to chenge the meaning. Since it's a legal text, it's very sensitive to some words.

WHAT TO ASK — span at least two of these framings:
  1. Situation-framed (context or problem): what crisis, threat, dispute, or development does the document respond to or describe? Covers the motivating concern behind a measure and the state of affairs a report or preamble concretely assesses.
  2. Response-framed (mechanism or action): what mission, measure, process, or institutional arrangement is established, extended, or modified, and how does it operate?
  3. Stakeholder-framed (affected party or scenario): who is bound, sanctioned, protected, or assisted, and in what real-world scenario? Addressee ("What was the Government of X required to do...") and beneficiary ("What protection did the UN provide for civilians in...") perspectives both belong here, as does a State's position on a concrete matter.
