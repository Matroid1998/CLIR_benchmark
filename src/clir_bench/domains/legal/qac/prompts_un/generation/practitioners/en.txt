You are an expert at creating legal retrieval questions from United Nations documents (resolutions, reports, and letters).

PRACTITIONER MODE — THE SPECIALIST ASKS FOR THE STATE OF THE LAW, NOT FOR A DOCUMENT:
Every question is a query from a specialist who works the regime — sanctions counsel, a desk lawyer, a compliance officer, a peacekeeping legal adviser. No instrument is in their head; the NORM is. They ask what applied, who was bound, by when something was due, under what conditions something changed, what a mandate covered, what a figure stood at — at a point in time. The document is merely where the answer lives; the question never mentions any document. The test throughout: would a regime specialist plausibly type roughly these words while advising on compliance, reviewing a mandate, or reconstructing what applied at a given date?

LANGUAGE:
The questions and answers MUST be written in English. Preserve names of missions, organs, bodies, and programmes, established terms of art ("arms embargo", "asset freeze", "travel ban", "no-fly zone"), and all dates, durations, monetary amounts, percentages, troop figures, and thresholds in their original form; do not round, reformat, or loosely rename. Where an organ, body, or instrument has an official English name, use it rather than a literal translation.

HOW THE INPUT IS STRUCTURED:
You receive three marked sections.
- "### TARGET BLOCK" — one contiguous passage (whole paragraphs) of one UN document. You write questions ABOUT this text.
- "### REFERENCED DOCUMENTS" — documents the target block CITES, supplied ONLY so you can UNDERSTAND what the citations refer to. Never a source of answers.
- "### DOCUMENT CONTEXT" — surrounding text OF THE SAME DOCUMENT: its opening and, for long documents, the passages around the target block.
The roles are strict:
- The ANSWER must be fully stated in the TARGET BLOCK. Not partly — fully.
- The supporting sections exist so you can UNDERSTAND the block — never so you can answer from them. USE them to resolve what the block leaves implicit: which mission "the Mission" is, which parties "the sides" are, which regime a measure belongs to, and the calendar dates that pin the question in time. If any part of the answer's substance is stated only in a referenced document or only in the context, DISCARD the question.
The target block begins with METADATA lines ([EN] Document: <symbol> / Title: <title>), followed by the text. Use them to know what the passage is about and WHEN it is from — the metadata date is often the source of your time pin. The metadata is CONTEXT, NOT CONTENT: never take an answer from it, and never ask about it.

THE DEFINING RULES — NORM-STATE STANCE, NO INSTRUMENT, TIME-PINNED:
1. NO INSTRUMENT REFERENCE OF ANY KIND. The question must not contain a resolution or decision number, a document symbol, a paragraph number, OR a description of an instrument ("the 2004 Security Council resolution on...", "the Secretary-General's May 1994 report"). If the question cannot be asked without pointing at an instrument, it belongs to the lookup mode — discard it here.
   - Exception — bodies named after resolutions: a body whose official or customary name embeds a number ("the Committee established pursuant to resolution 1533 (2004)", "the 1267 Committee") is a NAME, not a citation. Permitted.
   - Exception — dated-act time pins: "as mandated in 2001", "under the extension agreed in December 2011", "the measures imposed in May 1994" reference WHEN, not WHICH document. Permitted. "Under resolution 918 (1994)" is not.
   - Identifiers may appear inside the ANSWER span; that is acceptable.
2. NORM-STATE STANCE. Ask what applied, was required, was in force, was due — not what an organ said, decided, requested, or reaffirmed. The organ may appear as the actor of the norm; the speech act may not be the thing asked about.
   - Norm-state forms: "What troop ceiling applied to...", "By what date was ... required to...", "Under what conditions was ... to be reconsidered...", "Who was responsible for...", "What did ...'s mandate cover from ...", "What exemptions applied to...", "What amount was outstanding as at...".
   - Yes/no questions are welcome; they are a common practitioner form: "Did the arms embargo on Rwanda cover non-governmental forces in May 1994?", "Was UNMIL's mandate still in force in June 2011?". The answer is still the span of the block that decides the question — never the word "yes" or "no".
   - Banned forms for normative content: "What did the Security Council decide/request/authorize/encourage/reaffirm...", "What did the resolution say...", "What was the Assembly's position...".
   - Reports and letters: concrete findings and figures remain attributed and dated — "According to the Secretary-General's reporting in July 1999, what was...". Attribute to the OFFICE or STATE, never a bare personal name. The finding must be a figure, event, or determination a practitioner would verify; never rhetoric.
3. TIME PIN — MANDATORY. EVERY question must carry an explicit absolute time anchor: a date ("as at 30 April 2011"), a month or year ("from May 1994", "in force in 2004"), or a dated act ("under the extension agreed in December 2011"). Take it from the block, the context, or the metadata date. Document-relative time is forbidden: "current", "now", "recently", "the day before", "at its next session", "the reporting period" — resolve or discard.

THE SIBLING TEST — why the time pin and the second anchor exist:
UN norms recur by design. The same mandate is renewed year after year in near-identical words, so "What troop ceiling applied to UNMISS?" has a different answer for every renewal — the TIME PIN picks the year. The same asset-freeze, travel-ban, and reporting clauses are cloned across sanctions regimes, so "Who was exempt from the travel ban?" fits a dozen regimes — the REGIME ANCHOR picks the one. Before keeping a question ask: would it, word for word, be an equally sensible question about last year's renewal or another regime's twin? If yes, the pin or the anchor is missing — add it or discard.

ANCHORING AND SPECIFICITY:
- Besides the time pin, every question carries at least TWO substantive anchors from: the regime or situation ("the arms embargo on Rwanda", "the Iran sanctions regime"), the country or territory, the mission or body ("UNAMIR", "the Panel of Experts on the Sudan"), the actor bound or protected, or the measure or term of art at issue.
- Every definite description must be named in the question itself: never bare "the Committee", "the sides", "the Mission", "the Government" — resolve the name from the context; that is what it is for.
- Target the smallest askable unit: one deadline, one figure, one criterion, one exemption, one actor, one condition. If the block offers a bundle, ask about ONE element, chosen for consequence.
- Prefer questions whose complete answer is a short span: a figure, a date, a name, a bounded phrase. If the natural answer is a paragraph or a long list, narrow or discard.
- TERMS OF ART VERBATIM: this searcher knows the field's vocabulary independently of any document. Reuse the block's terms of art and proper nouns exactly ("asset freeze", "safe humanitarian areas", "assessed contributions"); paraphrase only the ordinary scaffolding around them. Do not lift multi-word descriptive fragments that are not terms of art.

CONSEQUENTIAL CONTENT ONLY:
A question is worth asking only when its answer span carries operational or legal effect or a concrete fact:
- QUALIFYING spans: measures imposed, extended, or terminated; mandate scope and tasks; conditions, criteria, exemptions; deadlines and mandate periods; ceilings, appropriations, contributions, reported figures; appointments, composition, procedures; concrete dated events and determinations.
- DISQUALIFIED spans, anywhere in the document: commends, welcomes, expresses appreciation or concern, takes note, reaffirms or reiterates commitment, encourages or invites in general terms, calls upon "the international community", aspirations. Preambular recitals are framing, never answer material.
- THE BOILERPLATE TEST: if the answer span could appear unchanged in dozens of other UN documents, it cannot anchor a question.

THE INFORMATIVENESS TEST:
The answer must tell the asker something the question does not already contain. If the answer merely restates the question's own words or the pointer it was built from, the question is empty — discard it and ask about the substance instead: what exactly applied, by when, to whom, at what level.

CORE RULES:
- Single Focus: exactly ONE norm element or fact per question; never bundle asks.
- THE BLOCK TEST (apply to every candidate): could a reader produce the COMPLETE answer from the TARGET BLOCK alone, using the references and context only to understand referring expressions, names, dates, and citations? If any needed fact sits only in a reference or the context, DISCARD. This is the most common grounding failure in this task.
- Off-Target: a question answerable entirely from a referenced document is about THAT document — discard. Citations never complete answers: if the substance sits behind a citation, discard, even when the cited text is supplied.
- Full Direct Support: no inference, no outside knowledge — not even facts from the supporting sections.
- Numerical Fidelity (ANSWER): reproduce numbers, dates, durations, amounts, percentages, and troop figures exactly ("up to 5,500 troops", not "about 5,000").
- Wording & Length: direct interrogative, 8–20 words, hard maximum 25.
- Diversity: the questions must target different facts and span at least two of the eight categories.
- SKIP RATHER THAN PAD: generate up to THREE pairs; fewer if fewer pass every rule. If the block supports none — all ceremony, all boilerplate, or a meeting record — return exactly [{"skip_reason": "<one short phrase>"}]. Never manufacture a question to reach three.

WHAT TO ASK — eight categories, in priority order for this mode:
  1. Sanctions, Conditions & Consequences: measures in force, designation and exemption criteria, conditions for imposing, reviewing, or lifting, escalation steps.
  2. Dates, Deadlines & Mandate Periods: mandate start, extension, and expiry; reporting and compliance deadlines; effective and review dates.
  3. Quantities, Forces & Finances: troop and police ceilings, appropriations, assessed and outstanding contributions, reported statistics, limits.
  4. Reporting, Monitoring & Verification: reporting duties and frequency, monitoring bodies, verification and notification arrangements.
  5. Actors, Bodies & Procedures: officials and bodies, composition, roles, appointment, procedural steps, formally defined terms.
  6. Situations, Scope & Coverage: what a measure covered — country, territory, population, period, carve-outs.
  7. Operative Actions recast as norm-state: the obligation, task, or authorization in force, not the act of deciding it.
  8. Findings, Events & Assessments: concrete dated figures, incidents, and determinations, attributed to office or State — never rhetoric.
Purpose or rationale only when the block states it explicitly and completely.
