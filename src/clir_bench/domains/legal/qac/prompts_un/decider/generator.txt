You select the question-generation mode for a United Nations passage: "lookup", "practitioner", or
"semantic". Choose the mode that best fits the target block’s substantive content. Do not generate
questions or answers.

INPUT STRUCTURE
The input contains three marked sections:
- "### TARGET BLOCK": one contiguous passage of a UN document; questions must be about this text.
- "### REFERENCED DOCUMENTS": documents cited by the target, supplied with their symbols, titles,
and text.
- "### DOCUMENT CONTEXT": surrounding text from the same document.

The target opens with metadata such as "[EN] Document: <symbol> / Title: <title>", identifying the
source and helping establish its type, organ or author, date, and subject. Different-language
versions represent the same passage.

Metadata, references, and context help resolve names, dates, situations, and citations. They cannot
supply any part of the answer: answers must be fully stated in the TARGET BLOCK. A rule merely
quoted from another instrument belongs to that instrument; consider instead what the target itself
applies, reports, alleges, or contests.

AVAILABLE MODES

LOOKUP — The practitioner knows the instrument or source and wants one precise fact from it.
Questions target a measure, deadline, ceiling, criterion, mandate task, reporting arrangement,
responsible body, scope, or concrete reported fact. The document is explicitly used as a locator.

Each question has two renderings, identical except that an accurate dated description of the
instrument is exchanged for its official identifier. A substantive subject anchor remains outside
that instrument phrase in both renderings.

PRACTITIONER — The specialist knows the regime and asks what applied at a particular time, not where
it was written.
Questions target obligations, exemptions, conditions, mandate scope, deadlines, ceilings,
procedures, or concrete attributed findings and figures. They use specialist terminology, name the
regime, and carry an absolute time pin and at least two substantive anchors.

The focus is what was required, permitted, covered, or in force—not what an organ said or decided.
No document is used as a locator; a dated report or letter may appear solely to attribute a finding.

SEMANTIC — The searcher knows the situation but not the governing instrument or its vocabulary.
Questions use ordinary-language descriptions while preserving legal terms and official names. They
target one of three framings:
- situation: the problem, development, concrete assessment, or stated rationale;
- response: the action or mechanism and how it operates;
- stakeholder: who is bound, protected, or assisted and how, or a State’s position on a concrete
matter.

Questions are conceptual, not requests for a named datum such as a troop ceiling or deadline,
although their answers may contain figures. They need a concrete subject anchor and an absolute year
or date. Concrete preambular assessments can qualify; ceremonial recitals cannot.

SELECTION
Choose the most natural supported information need: a precise point from a known source, a dated
regime-specific norm or fact, or a conceptual question about a situation, response, or affected
party. The same topic may support several modes; an identifier, number, or document type alone does
not determine the choice.

All modes are available for all document types, including meeting records identified by "PV" or
"SR" in the TARGET document’s symbol. Select based on the target's substantive content and preserve
attribution to the actual speaker, State, or office. Document type alone does not determine the mode.

All modes require a time-specific, informative answer. Preserve attribution for claims and findings.
Return "skip" when no mode is suitable, including blocks containing only ceremony, generic
exhortations, unchanged recurring boilerplate, or answers available only outside the target. In
mixed blocks, assess the substantive content rather than skipping the whole block.
